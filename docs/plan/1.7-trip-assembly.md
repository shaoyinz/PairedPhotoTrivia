# 1.7 Trip assembly (`trips.py`)

**Status (2026-10-04):** done — `spike/tests/test_trips.py`, no real library needed. On the
synthetic fixture, now with six planted trips (see the last items), §1.4's 71 matched keys give
seven away windows and six trips:

- Napa and Santa Cruz: A 4, B 4 each.
- Monterey: A 8, B 1. That is every photo A took that day, gathered around B's one matched photo.
- Tahoe: **one** window over three days and two nights with no photos (A 19, B 16).
- Yosemite: A 14, B 1, over **two** days. B's one photo is on the first, and A's tail carries the
  trip into the second ([Tails](#tails-2026-10-04)).
- Mendocino: A 6, B 4, on Saturday only. B is home on Sunday morning, so A's Sunday alone stays out.

The Half Moon Bay near-trip is in town with 2 + 2 photos, and the ≥ 5 rule drops it.
`photoquiz trips` writes `data/trips.json` and also lists the away windows the ≥ 5 rule dropped.
The walking skeleton now stops at §1.8 (`report`).

## How a trip is assembled

1. Keep the matched buckets that are away ([§1.6](1.6-away-rule.md)), each tested at its geohash-6 cell center
   against the homes both partners had at that hour ([§1.5](1.5-anchors.md)'s home history).
2. Sessionize them: a gap of more than 6 h starts a new session.
3. Join out-of-town sessions across the nights between them. **A trip ends when either partner
   is seen back in their home city, not when the photos pause.** The home city is everything
   within 25 km of the partner's home→work segment.
4. Carry each out-of-town end along photos only one partner took: its **tail**. It runs until
   either partner is seen back in their home city, or that partner's photos pause for more than
   24 h ([Tails](#tails-2026-10-04)).
5. Widen each trip by 3 h on each side, backfill both partners' photos, and keep it if it holds ≥ 5.

A bucket that was home then but is away now, at a home one of you has since left, goes into an
old-home day instead (see below).

| Parameter | Default | Role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `gap_hours` | 6 | Within a day: away buckets further apart start a new session |
| `city_km` | 25 | Home city: km around home→work. Ends trips; never decides what is away |
| `max_silence_hours` | 72 | A trip also ends once each partner has gone this long without a located photo |
| `tail_gap_hours` | 24 | A tail goes on while one partner's next photo out of their own home city is this close; 0 = off |
| `reach_hours` | 3 | Backfill reach past a trip's first and last hour; at most `gap_hours / 2` |
| `min_photos` | 5 | Across both devices, after backfill |

On the fixture, every `city_km` from 15 to 50 with any `max_silence_hours` of 24 or more gives
the six trips above. A cap of 12 h splits Tahoe by night again. With `city_km = 5`, home is
only the commute buffer, and from 72 h Napa and Santa Cruz merge. A `tail_gap_hours` of 24 to 72
carries Yosemite into its second day. At 12 h the night stops it, and at 0 there are no tails.

## Why it changed (2026-10-02)

The first build followed this plan as first written: sessionize every matched bucket with a
6 h gap, then keep a session if any bucket is away. The fixture exposed two problems that no
single gap could fix:

- **A multi-day trip split by night.** Nobody shoots overnight, and Tahoe's overnight gap is
  19 h. Every gap from 3 to 18 h gave Tahoe as three windows for one label.
- **Longer gaps chained home buckets into trips.** From a 15 h gap, the near-trip's session took
  in the next night's matched home bucket. It then passed ≥ 5 on home photos, which made it a
  false positive.

Sessions from away buckets only, with a 19–28 h gap, fixed the fixture, but only within that
narrow band. Ending a trip on evidence of being home does not hinge on a tuned gap. The evidence
area is the home city rather than the 5 km buffer: dinner across town, an errand or a friend's
place should count as back. The iPhone's own Trips collection was considered and ruled out.
PhotoKit has no API for it, Moments have been deprecated since iOS 13, and either would see one
partner's library only.

## Checklist

- [x] Sessionize the away buckets by time gap: a gap > 6 h starts a new session (tunable).
      Start here rather than with ST-DBSCAN — it is deterministic, trivially portable to Swift,
      and one parameter.
      *`sessionize(keys, gap_hours=6)`. The gap is the `hour_index` difference between
      consecutive keys, so hours 10 and 16 share a session and hours 10 and 17 do not. Keys are
      sorted by (hour, geohash). `BucketKey`'s own ordering compares geohash first, so it would
      be wrong here. DBSCAN is not built. It would not fix the night split either: a night with
      no photos looks the same at any time scale.*
- [x] Only away buckets enter a trip
      *A bucket is tested at its geohash-6 cell center (`cell_center`). Both devices hold the
      matched key, so both get the same answer without anyone's photo coordinates. A cell is at
      most 1.2 × 0.6 km, small against the 5 km buffer. Home buckets never join a trip, so they
      can no longer stretch a trip's window over home time.*
- [x] Join out-of-town sessions across nights; end the trip on home-city evidence or silence
      *`join_nights`. "Out of town" uses the away rule with the 25 km cities in place of the
      5 km buffers: outside both cities with a shared home, outside either with different
      homes. In-town sessions never join, because you sleep at home, so a local date stays its
      own window. Two out-of-town sessions join unless, between them, either partner took a GPS
      photo inside their own home city. The one exception is a partner whose own city holds the
      trip's out-of-town part: when B visits A, A's photos at home are the visit, not evidence.
      Photos without GPS neither end a trip nor keep it going. A trip also ends unless at least
      one partner kept taking located photos, never 72 h without one. Each partner's silence is
      timed alone, so each phone can answer for itself. Two partners each shooting every 80 h,
      40 h apart, would cover each other together, but the trip still splits.*
- [x] Keep a trip only if total photos across both devices ≥ 5
      *Counted after backfill, as the §1.1.5 stub said. Monterey has only 4 matched photos
      (A 3, B 1), so A's unmatched ones are what make it a trip.*
- [x] Backfill: once a trip survives, include every photo from both partners inside its window,
      including unmatched and GPS-less ones (covers "only one partner shot that day"; the days
      after, when only one partner shot, are for the tails below)
      *`window(trip)` covers the first to the last matched hour, plus 3 h on each side. Without
      the reach, Monterey's window would be the single hour of B's photo: it would miss A's
      11:00 and 15:00 shots and, with them, the trip. The reach also covers A's matched photos,
      which can sit an hour either side of their key. `assemble` refuses a reach over half the
      gap, so two windows never overlap and no photo lands in two trips. The reach used to be
      half the gap, but at long gaps it grew into home photos, so it is now its own parameter.
      The cost is that home photos within reach of a trip's ends count too.*
- [x] Output `data/trips.json`: window start/end UTC, matched bucket count, photo counts per
      partner, representative geohash, away/shared-home reason
      *`TripWindow` as it stands. `start_utc`/`end_utc` are the half-open window in epoch
      seconds. `away_reason` names the rule, where the trip was and how many sessions it
      joined, e.g. `shared home: out of town, 3 sessions`. The place is one or more of
      `out of town`, `in town`, `in a's city` and `in b's city`; the last two only occur with
      different homes, and `in b's city` means A visiting B. The representative geohash is the
      out-of-town cell with the most matched hours. Ties go to the earliest, then the smallest.*
- [x] A fixture case that tells home-city ending from home-buffer ending
      *Napa (Sat), then A's dinner in Oakland (Sun, ~15 km out: past both 5 km buffers, inside
      the 25 km city), then Santa Cruz (Mon). Both trips are labeled. It sits before the daily
      noise starts and uses its own random stream, so every earlier photo, ID and §1.3–1.6 count
      is unchanged. Tahoe already covers nights with no photos.*

## Old-home days (2026-10-04)

A home you have left holds memories too, so everyday life there is quiz material rather than
excluded (PRD). Each matched bucket is now tested twice: against the homes of its hour, and
against today's homes. Away then: a trip, assembled as above with the home cities of the time,
so a trip taken while living elsewhere ends back in that city. Home then but away now: an
old-home day. Home both times: dropped, as before.

- [x] `Homes.of(anchors_a, anchors_b, eras_a=..., eras_b=...)`: the buffers, home cities and
      shared-home rule in force at any hour. *Each partner's moves are their own, so the pair
      changes setting at either partner's move. While both homes are confirmed ones, the anchors
      files' `shared_home` override applies; once either is an inferred past home, sharing goes by
      distance (< 1 km), since nobody confirmed it. Without eras it is the anchors alone, and
      every earlier result stands.*
- [x] Old-home buckets are sessionized like trips (gap > 6 h) and **never joined across nights**, so
      each is at most about a day, as in-town sessions already were. Same reach, backfill and ≥ 5
      rule. *`kind = "old_home"` in `trips.json`, reason `old home: shared home then`.*
- [x] Where an old-home day's window would overlap a trip's, the trip wins and the day is dropped,
      so no photo lands in both. Trip candidates count before the ≥ 5 rule, so lowering
      `min_photos` never brings an old-home day back.
- [x] Trip reasons gain "then" when measured against a past home, e.g.
      `shared home then: out of town, 1 session`. A gap between sessions is judged with the homes
      at its start.
- [x] `photoquiz trips --no-past-homes` falls back to the anchors alone, for comparison.

Known limit: a past home seen in one partner's library only. If both lived in the old city but B's
library shows too few evenings there to infer it, A's eras say shared home while B's say B lived at
today's home. The pair then counts as different homes, and old-city weekends become visits, so
trips again. Real labels show whether this happens.

## Tails (2026-10-04)

The backfill reaches only 3 h past a trip's last matched hour. Say B takes one photo on the
first day and none after, while A shoots two more days. Those days are lost: `join_nights`
only joins two matched sessions, so A's photos alone can keep a trip from ending on silence
but cannot carry it on. The same holds before the first match. Each out-of-town end of a trip
now gets a tail:

1. Only an end whose session has a bucket out of town gets one. In town you sleep at home, as
   for joining.
2. A tail follows one partner's GPS photos outside **their own** home city (25 km), not outside
   the pair's. Under the pair rule, after B visits A, all of A's everyday photos at home would
   count as away, and the tail would never stop.
3. Each partner's run is timed alone. It starts at the edge of the matched hours, and each
   photo must come within 24 h of the one before.
4. Once either partner is seen back in their own home city, both tails stop before that hour.
   The host exception holds as for joining: when the trip is in A's city, A's photos at home
   neither carry nor stop it.
5. GPS-less photos neither carry nor stop a tail. Backfill still picks them up inside the
   window.
6. The farther of the two runs counts. Then come the 3 h reach, backfill and ≥ 5, as before.

**Why 24 h, not the 72 h silence cap.** Joining has a match at both ends, so the far end proves
they are together again, and waiting 72 h is safe. A tail has a match at one end only. At 72 h,
A's next solo work trip would be glued on whenever A takes no photos at home in between. 24 h
still covers a night with no photos (Tahoe's is 19 h).

**Left out on purpose.** Comparing where the two partners' photos are, such as "B's photo is far
from A's, so they split up", would mean sending coordinates across devices. Requiring tail photos
to stay near the trip's place would cut off road trips.

- [x] `tails(trips, homes, ps_a=..., ps_b=..., tail_gap_hours=24)` → each trip's tails in hours,
      (before its first match, after its last). `assemble` widens the window by them before the
      reach. *Each partner first says in which hour they were first back in their own city.
      The nearer of the two hours is the cutoff. Each then carries the end through hours before
      the cutoff, as far as their own run goes. All of it is in whole hours, the bucket
      resolution. Judged with the home cities in force at the matched edge, as a gap is judged
      with those at its start. `tail_gap_hours = 0` turns tails off.*
- [x] Neighbouring windows never overlap. *Cores are more than `gap_hours ≥ 2 · reach` apart, but
      two tails could meet. Say A carries the first trip on and B carries the next one back, and
      each partner pauses more than 72 h, so `join_nights` keeps them apart. A tail stops
      2 · reach short of the next trip's first matched hour. A head stops as far after the
      previous trip's final tail. Old-home days still give way to the wider trip windows.*
- [x] `TripWindow.tail_hours_before` / `tail_hours_after` in `trips.json`. The matched part is
      the window less these. `photoquiz trips` prints them, and `--tail-gap-hours 0` turns
      them off. *Phase 2 can then ask about the tail alone ("Were you two together through
      Mar 7?") instead of the whole trip.*
- [x] Fixture: Yosemite (B's one photo on Saturday, A shoots through Sunday) and Mendocino
      (together Saturday, B home Sunday morning, A stays on alone). *Placed before Napa, with a
      random stream of their own, so every earlier photo is unchanged. One photo of A at home
      on the Wednesday between stands for the everyday photos a real library holds there. Without
      it, a 168 h silence cap joins the two weekends, because the fixture has no photos at all
      before April. Without B's Sunday photo at home, A's Sunday would be carried in.*
- [x] Scored in [§1.8](1.8-evaluation.md) as tail hours inside and outside labels, since
      precision cannot see a tail that runs on.

Known limits:

- **B goes home first and takes no located photo there, while A stays on**, say a holiday that
  runs into a work trip. A's days alone then count as together. The same goes at the start when
  B joins late and took no photo before leaving. The backstops are product ones: A approves
  every quiz photo, and in phase 2 tier 3 can confirm the tail alone.
- **A whole day with no photos mid-trip** (more than 24 h between photos) ends the tail there.
  The days after are lost unless a match brings the trip back.
- Where two tails would meet, the earlier trip's tail goes first.

## For phase 2

Every test reduces to per-partner answers:

- per matched bucket: is it in my buffer then and now, and in my home city;
- per gap between two out-of-town sessions: was I seen back in my city, and did I keep taking
  located photos;
- per trip end: in which hour was I first back in my city; then, given the nearer of the two
  partners' hours, how far my own photos out of my city carry the end.

So each phone answers for itself, and neither needs the other's anchors or photos. The gap bits
do tell the partner something new: that you were home at some point in that gap. That is small
next to what the shared salt already reveals, but it belongs in the partner-privacy decision.
The tail answers are hours, not bits. The partner learns the hour of your last photo out of
town and of your first photo back home. That is at the buckets' own resolution, and the trip's
dates are shared anyway, but it belongs in the same decision.
